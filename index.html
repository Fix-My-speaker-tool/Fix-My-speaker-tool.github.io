The Science: How Sound Pressure Waves Displace Liquid

Modern mobile phones, smartwatches, and wireless earbuds utilize water-resistant seals (IP67 or IP68 ratings) to protect interior processors. However, physical speaker cones must remain mechanically exposed to the air to project sound.

When exposed to water, liquid surface tension forms an elastic barrier across the micro-mesh. The trapped water acts as a damper on the driver cone, causing severely muffled, distorted sound. The Fix My Speaker tool generates rhythmic, pulsed sound waves calibrated at 165 Hz. At this exact physical resonance, the voice coil drives the speaker diaphragm to its peak excursion limits. This piston-like movement creates high-pressure air bursts that rupture the water's surface tension, physically ejecting drops out through the grill.
